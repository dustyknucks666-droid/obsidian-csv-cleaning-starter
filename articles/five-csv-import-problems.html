Five CSV Import Problems That Cause Silent Business Data Errors

By Dustin Rebellato Technical Guide Reading time: ~6 minutes

The comma-separated values (CSV) format remains the global lingua franca of business data transfer. Inventory catalogs, bookkeeping entries, customer records, and banking logs are constantly exported and imported in CSV format.

Yet because CSV is an unstandardized, untyped plain-text representation (despite RFC 4180), software parsers regularly make destructive assumptions. Here are the five most damaging defects that corrupt commercial databases.

1. Silent Truncation of Leading Zeroes

When Excel or standard automated scripts open a CSV without strict schema definitions, they evaluate every column for apparent data types. If a cell contains 04102 (a Massachusetts ZIP code) or 0098412 (a barcode SKU), the software interprets it as a number and strips the leading zeros:

  • 04102 becomes 4102 (an invalid ZIP code).
  • 0098412 becomes 98412 (mismatched SKU causing warehouse fulfillment errors).

Once the spreadsheet is saved, the leading zeros are permanently gone from the text stream.

2. Calendar Date Ambiguity & Transposition

Dates written with forward slashes such as 04/05/2026 have zero inherent locale specification. Depending on whether your spreadsheet engine or importing database uses US formatting (MM/DD/YYYY) or Commonwealth / Canadian formatting (DD/MM/YYYY):

  • US parser reads: April 5, 2026
  • Canadian parser reads: May 4, 2026

The error is silent: no crash occurs, but payment due dates, delivery commitments, or financial accounting quarters are corrupted by an entire month. Standardizing strictly to ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD) before import is essential.

3. Unescaped Commas & Line Breaks Breaking Row Boundaries

Under RFC 4180, fields containing commas or line breaks (such as product descriptions or multi-line shipping addresses) must be enclosed in quotation marks. If a text field contains an unescaped comma or an unclosed quotation mark:

  • The comma acts as a delimiter, shifting every subsequent column to the right.
  • A customer's telephone number shifts into the price column; their address shifts into the tax column.
  • An unclosed quote causes the parser to consume subsequent rows as part of the previous row's text.

4. Character Encoding Mismatches (UTF-8 vs Windows-1252)

When exports containing accented characters, French names (common across Ottawa and Gatineau), or currency symbols (€, £, é, à) are saved using Windows-1252 or ANSI encoding and ingested into a UTF-8 system, mojibake occurs:

  • Currency symbols turn into question marks (?250.00) or corrupt numeric parsing.

5. Scientific Notation on Long Numeric Identifiers

Credit card numbers, bank transit codes, IMEI numbers, and 12-digit UPC barcodes exceed the standard 11-digit integer display of standard spreadsheet programs. A barcode 123456789012 is automatically rendered as 1.23457E+11. If saved back to disk, the trailing precision digits are replaced with zeroes, permanently corrupting the identifier.
